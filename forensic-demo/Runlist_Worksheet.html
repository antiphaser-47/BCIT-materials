THE RUN LIST  ·  the fourteen bytes you just decoded
Four rows down, exactly as promised.
  1. Find 80 00 00 00 in the record. That byte is your starting point.
  2. Count two rows down. The right half of that row is the allocated size.
  3. The next row down begins with the real (logical) size — the first 8 bytes.
  4. One more row and you are at the run list. That is your check that you counted right.
Two things that catch people here.
1. These are 8-byte fields holding fairly small numbers, so the last five bytes are 00. Reverse only the bytes that are not zero: 00 40 06 00 00 00 00 00 → 0x064000 → 409,600. (Not this file's number — just the method.)
2. Allocated and real sit one row apart and look nearly the same. Taking the wrong one is the single most common way to fail the hash. Real is the smaller of the two.

You can work out the allocated size two ways — clusters × 4,096, and by reading it at +0x28. They must agree. If they do, you are looking in the right place, and the number below it is the one you want.
